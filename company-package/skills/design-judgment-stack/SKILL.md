# Design judgment stack

## Outcome
Produce a fake-door, landing page or campaign surface that is credible, coherent, proposition-specific and visually intentional without confusing design quality with demand evidence.

## Operating model
Treat design guidance as an advisory stack, not a pile of prompts. Separate the job into mechanisms: aesthetic direction, interaction/craft execution, reusable project constraints, reference retrieval and audit. Select only the mechanisms required by the brief and preserve disagreements between source rules instead of silently blending them.

When the optional Gogh upstream is available, consult its current operating guidance and dated source state before relying on specific rules. Do not copy stale numeric claims, version assumptions or contested rules into Founder OS as fact.

## Workflow
1. Read the experiment brief first: target segment, problem, offer, CTA, desired action, traffic source, brand constraints, legal copy and measurement plan.
2. Classify the design ambition. Decide whether the job is primarily preservation/refresh, evolution or a materially new direction.
3. Write a short direction commitment before implementation. It must name the intended visual direction, one signature element, what should be memorable after a few seconds, and the generic/default pattern being avoided.
4. If direction is uncertain or the brief calls for a major change, generate multiple distinct directions before selecting one. Do not spend iteration budget polishing the first arbitrary output.
5. Keep immutable source/reference material separate from derived guidance. Record where important design rules or references came from and when they were checked.
6. Build under the chosen direction. Craft decisions such as type, spacing, motion, density, hierarchy and imagery should support that direction and the experiment's conversion goal.
7. Run mechanical review first: usability, responsive behavior, accessibility basics, CTA visibility, copy accuracy, route/form preservation and obvious AI-generated defects.
8. Run a distinctiveness review after mechanical quality passes. Ask whether the page has a recognizable visual thesis/signature or whether the same layout could be handed to a competitor by swapping logo and copy.
9. For redesigns, compare against the incumbent surface and verify that URLs, navigation, forms, required legal copy and non-negotiable brand elements were not silently changed.
10. Record the selected direction, rejected alternatives, material review findings and final approval rationale with the experiment artifacts.

## Evidence discipline
Design audit results are design evidence only. They can justify statements such as "the page passed the defined review" but never statements such as "customers want the product." Market validation still requires observed prospect behavior through the owning validation agents.

## Completion rule
A surface is not complete because it looks polished. It is complete for design purposes only when its direction is explicit, the implementation satisfies the experiment brief, mechanical issues are cleared, meaningful distinctiveness is demonstrated or deliberately not required, preservation constraints are respected, and the result is ready to be measured as part of a real validation experiment.
